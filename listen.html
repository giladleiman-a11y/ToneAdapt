Tonebench · Experimental lab

Hear what your amp is doing.

Use your iPhone microphone to inspect the sound coming from your Marshall MG15R. This is a measurement assistant, not an AI that can magically identify the original studio tone.

01 / Setup

Record a consistent test

Put your phone 30–60 cm in front of the amp, keep it still, and play the same riff or chords at the same volume for each test. Use a comfortable volume; don't turn the amp up for the microphone.

Microphone off. Nothing is recorded or uploaded.

Values are relative spectral energy percentages, not actual amp knob positions. Compare two recordings of the same riff for more useful results.

What this can and cannot do
  • Can: compare bass, mid and treble energy, show a live spectrum and suggest cautious EQ experiments.
  • Cannot: identify the exact gain knob position, reliably measure distortion or sustain from one phone recording, recognize the song, or match a studio recording automatically.
  • Important: phone microphones, room reflections, distance, notes played and iOS processing all change the spectrum. A bright reading does not necessarily mean your Treble knob is wrong.
  • Privacy: analysis runs in your browser. No recording is stored or sent to a server.
